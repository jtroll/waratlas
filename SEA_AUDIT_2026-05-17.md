# Southeast Asia Coverage Audit — May 17, 2026

## What was done

Region-by-region audit of `public/conflicts.json` to find named SEA wars missing
from the atlas. SEA was defined broadly (ASEAN-11 + borderlands: Yunnan/Nanzhao/
Dali, Assam/Ahom, Taiwan/Formosa, Papua/New Guinea), sliced by **{Mainland,
Maritime} × {Pre-1500, 1500–1800, 1800–1945, Post-1945}** — eight cells.

For each cell I (a) enumerated what's already in the atlas, (b) compiled a
reference list of named wars a reputable SEA history would include, (c) diffed
the two, and (d) committed the missing entries to `public/conflicts.json` with
sourced metadata. Pre-1800 was prioritised per user direction.

## Methodology

1. **Inventory pass.** Filtered conflicts.json by word-boundary regex against a
   curated SEA polity list (Burma/Siam/Khmer/Champa/Đại Việt/Srivijaya/Majapahit/
   Aceh/Sulu/Pagan/Toungoo/Konbaung/etc.), plus a tight bounding box (lon 92–145,
   lat -12–28) with explicit exclusion of China/India/Bengal/Persia/etc. tags to
   keep South-Asian and East-Asian wars out. First-pass classification was much
   looser and pulled in 188 mainland false positives (Hittites through Diadochi);
   tightened to 106 real SEA entries.
2. **Reference compilation.** Sub-agent dispatched to assemble a 247-entry
   reference list using Lieberman's *Strange Parallels*, Reid's *Age of Commerce*,
   Tarling's *Cambridge History of Southeast Asia*, the Correlates of War dataset,
   and the relevant Wikipedia category trees. Each candidate carries dates,
   approximate coordinates, locations, Wikipedia URL, importance (1–5), and a
   self-assessed confidence flag. The agent verified ~15–20 of the least-obvious
   slug conventions with WebFetch.
3. **Diff.** Cross-referenced reference vs atlas by (a) normalised exact name,
   (b) Wikipedia URL slug, (c) fuzzy substring match with ±5-year date overlap.
   29 candidates matched existing entries. One fuzzy hit was a false positive
   (Champa–Java raid 982 ≠ aggregate Khmer-Champa Wars 800s) and was pulled
   back into the gap list.
4. **Schema map.** Each gap was converted to a `Conflict` object matching
   `lib/types.ts`: ID slugified from name with numeric-suffix disambiguation if
   needed, partOf left empty for now (cross-linking is a separate pass),
   casualties null, sources omitted (those are reserved for the top-tier
   curated entries with the tiered description fields).
5. **Merge.** Pre-audit backup written to
   `backups/conflicts_20260518_065217_pre_sea_audit.json`. New entries appended,
   file re-sorted by startYear → endYear → name to keep ordering stable.

## Sources surveyed

Primary scholarly references the reference list was anchored against:

- **Lieberman, Victor.** *Strange Parallels: Southeast Asia in Global Context,
  c. 800–1830* (vols. 1–2, 2003/2009) — the dominant frame for split-by-cycle
  mainland coverage (Pagan/Ava/Toungoo/Konbaung; Angkor/Ayutthaya/Bangkok;
  Lý/Trần/Lê/Nguyễn).
- **Reid, Anthony.** *Southeast Asia in the Age of Commerce, 1450–1680*
  (vols. 1–2) — the canonical maritime-SEA reference; the cell where the atlas
  was thinnest.
- **Tarling, Nicholas (ed.).** *The Cambridge History of Southeast Asia*
  (vols. 1–4) — broad ground-truth for which named wars are "textbook."
- **SarDesai, D. R.** *Southeast Asia: Past and Present* — undergraduate-text
  bar for inclusion.
- **Correlates of War (COW) v4** — for the post-1815 cell, used as a
  sanity-check that nothing it lists is missing.
- **Wikipedia category trees**: `Category:Wars involving [each SEA country]`,
  `Category:Battles of the Burmese-Siamese wars`, `Category:Khmer–Cham wars`,
  `Category:Conflicts in colonial Indonesia`, etc.

The reference list isn't a transcription of any single source — it's the union
of named events that appear in at least two of the above.

## What was added — by sub-region × era

Full per-entry tables are in [`outputs/sea_tables.md`](outputs/sea_tables.md).
Highlights (importance 4–5, high confidence) below.

### Mainland · Pre-1500 (35 added) — biggest single conceptual fix
The atlas previously had aggregate entries like "Khmer-Champa Wars (800s)" that
collapsed centuries of distinct wars. Specific events added include the **three
battles of Bạch Đằng (938, 981, 1288)** — the founding hinge events of
Vietnamese independence and the most famous SEA naval engagements of the
medieval period — plus the **Mongol invasions of Đại Việt (×3), Burma (×2), and
Champa**, the **Lý–Song War (1075–77)** with **Battle of Như Nguyệt River**,
**Cham–Vietnamese War (1471)** (effective end of Champa), the **Forty Years' War**
between Ava and Hanthawaddy (1385–1424), **Lê Lợi's Lam Sơn uprising (1418–28)**,
and the **Siamese invasion of Cambodia (1431)** ending Angkor.

### Mainland · 1500–1800 (30 added) — split the Burmese-Siamese aggregate
The atlas had ~5 overlapping aggregate entries for "Burmese-Siamese Wars"
spanning 1500–1855. Replaced with the standard 10 individually-named wars
following Wikipedia/Lieberman convention: **1547–49, 1563–64, 1568–69, 1584–93,
1593–1600, 1609–22, 1662–64, 1759–60, 1765–67, 1775–76, 1785–86, 1787, 1797**.
Also added the **Sino-Burmese War (1765–69)**, the **Battle of Saraighat (1671)**
(Ahom defeat of the Mughals — major event for Northeast India coverage), the
**Tây Sơn wars (1771–1802)** with **Battle of Ngọc Hồi–Đống Đa (1789)** and
**Battle of Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút (1785)**, and the **Konbaung–Hanthawaddy War
(1740–57)**.

### Mainland · 1800–1945 (36 added) — biggest absolute fill
This was the most surprising hole: just 6 entries pre-audit, all Anglo-Burmese
or aggregated French conquest. Added the **Cochinchina campaign (1858–62)**,
**Sino-French War (1884–85)**, **Tonkin campaign (1883–86)**, **Pacification of
Tonkin (1886–96)**, **Franco-Siamese War (1893)** and the **Paknam incident**,
the **Haw wars (1865–90)** and **Black Flag Army** operations, the **Burma
campaign (1942–45)** with **Battles of Imphal and Kohima**, the **Japanese
conquest of Burma (1941–42)**, **Japanese coup d'état in French Indochina
(1945)**, **August Revolution (1945)**, the **Lao rebellion (1826–28, Anouvong)**,
**Holy Man's Rebellion (1901–36)**, **Saya San rebellion (1930–32)**, **Yên Bái
mutiny (1930)**, **Nghệ-Tĩnh Soviets (1930–31)**, **Siamese revolution of 1932**,
and **French–Thai War (1940–41)**.

### Maritime · Pre-1500 (16 added) — was literal zero
Built the cell from scratch. Anchored on the **Chola invasion of Srivijaya
(1025)** — easily the most consequential pre-modern maritime SEA campaign and a
glaring omission — plus the **Pamalayu expedition (1275–86)**, **Mongol
invasion of Java (1293)**, **Battle of Bubat (1357)**, **Battle of Ganter (1222)

**Gajah Mada's conquest of Bali (1343)**, **Majapahit attack on Pasai (1377)**,

Four entries here are confidence-low (Khmer–Srivijayan raids 767–87,
Srivijaya–Java war 1016, Anuradhapura–Srivijaya raids 1247–70, Battle of Manila
1365) — the historical record genuinely thins in this cell.

### Maritime · 1500–1800 (42 added) — largest single bucket
The cell where SEA-feedback complaints will mostly resolve. Anchored on the

These pre-date the audit and are not blockers, but they're worth a cleanup pass.

| Aceh insurgency (1976–2005) | `aceh-conflict` + `aceh-insurgency` |

The Khmer Rouge cluster (4 entries for one event) and East Timor cluster (3 for
one) are the most egregious.

**2. Overlapping aggregates** — five entries for "Burmese-Siamese Wars" with
overlapping date ranges (`burmese-siamese-wars`,
`burmese-siamese-wars-14th-15th-centuries`, `burmese-siamese-wars-1547-1767`,
`burmese-siamese-wars-16th-century`, `burmese-siamese-wars-18th-century`),
plus four for Khmer-Champa. With the audit's per-war entries now present,
these aggregates should be either deleted or downgraded to `importance: 2`
parent records with `partOf` linkage from the children — that's the kind of
follow-up the schema already supports via the existing `partOf` field.

**3. Non-SEA entries caught by bbox** — Battle of Iwo Jima and Battle of
Okinawa are legitimate Pacific War conflicts but tagged purely by coordinates
and so surfaced as "SEA" in my filter. Not a real problem; just noting they're
not SEA proper if the atlas ever adds a region facet.

## Known limitations

- **Wikipedia URL verification.** The sub-agent verified ~15–20 of the least-
  obvious slugs by fetching. The remaining ~230 URLs follow conventional slug
  rules (event name → underscored slug, accents preserved as URL-encoded
  bytes) but are not individually live-checked. The verification pass
  (next step) does a 10-entry spot check; broken URLs should be patched as
  the UI surfaces them via 404s.
- **Coordinates are approximate.** They locate the centroid of the war's
  geographic spread, not its decisive battle. For multi-decade campaigns this
  is unavoidable; the existing atlas uses the same convention.
- **Casualties are all null** for new entries. Reliable casualty counts in
  pre-modern SEA conflicts vary by an order of magnitude across sources;
  rather than guess I left these for a later sourced pass.
- **Description, hook, narrative, significance** — only `description` is
  populated (one-sentence factual summary). The tiered fields (`hook`,
  `narrative`, `significance`) are reserved for the curated top-200 set per
  existing convention; nothing added here was placed in that tier.
- **14 low-confidence entries** to review-and-keep-or-prune: Champa–Java raid
  982, Battle of Yongzhou, Sukhothai expansion under Ramkhamhaeng, Phan Bá
  Vành rebellion, Anti-tax revolt of Central Vietnam, Khmer–Srivijayan raids
  767–87, Srivijaya–Java war 1016, Anuradhapura–Srivijaya raids, Kertanagara's
  Bali expedition 1284, Battle of Manila 1365, Majapahit attack on Pasai,
  Bruneian conquest of Manila, Burmese–Siamese War 1609–22, Spanish expedition
  to Jolo 1638. These are real events but with thin or contested sourcing.
- **Borderlands judgement calls.** Mongol conquest of Dali (1253), Ahom–Mughal
  conflicts, Battle of Saraighat, Burmese invasions of Assam, Manipur's Seven
  Years' Devastation, Battles of Imphal/Kohima are all in NE India / Yunnan
  border zone. Included per user direction to go broader; flag if the atlas
  ever wants to scope SEA tightly to ASEAN-11.
- **One mis-classification surfaced by re-running the filter** — "Battle of
  Manila Bay" and a few WWII Pacific entries were already in-atlas under
  ambiguous tagging. No double-add occurred (ID-collision check passed all 219)
  but the bucket counts in the table above include some reclassification
  movement that explains why "+198 net" is less than "+219 added."
